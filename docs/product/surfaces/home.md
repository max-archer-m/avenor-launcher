# Home Interaction Specification

> Public semantic source: English. Chinese counterpart: [home.zh-CN.md](home.zh-CN.md). Shared navigation is defined in [navigation.md](../navigation.md); double-tap locking is defined in [double-tap-lock.md](../features/double-tap-lock.md); exact Home visual values are defined in the [Home presentation specification](../presentation/home.md); shared visual principles are defined in [design-foundations.md](../design-foundations.md); the complete spatial sketches are the shared [normal Home](../wireframes/home.txt) and [Home edit mode](../wireframes/home-edit-mode.txt) wireframes, with reading rules in the [wireframe index](../low-fidelity-wireframes.md).

## Purpose and structure

Home is the Launcher primary surface. It is one fixed, non-pageable, non-collapsible surface without folders. The page itself does not scroll; only populated vertical favorite lists can scroll independently under the overflow rules below. Its content is arranged from top to bottom:

1. Basic information region: time, then date and weekday.
2. Favorite-list area: up to two equal-status vertical favorite lists containing accepted saved launchable entries.
3. Secondary favorites area: a conditional module below the favorite-list area.

Home uses content-driven vertical sizing rather than fixed proportional regions and never scrolls vertically as a whole. Home first resolves the platform `safeDrawing` bounds and the content rectangle defined by the Home presentation specification. The basic-information region wraps its required content and interaction targets. When the natural favorite-list and populated-secondary heights fit within the remaining content height, both use their natural heights and no favorite list scrolls vertically. On combined overflow, the secondary favorites area retains its natural height and the favorite-list area uses the remaining viewport above it; only an overflowing favorite list scrolls vertically inside that viewport. The secondary favorites area uses the complete Home content width and has zero height when empty. Each favorite bar handles its own horizontal overflow without moving other Home regions. Remaining screen space is unallocated transparent Home space, not a persistent product region.

The favorite-list area supports at most two persisted vertical lists. One list uses the complete favorite-list-area width; two divide that width equally after the presentation-defined gap. Width is derived from visible-list count rather than a fixed percentage, so this rule does not authorize a third list. Both lists have equal product status, share one viewport height, preserve independent content and scroll state, and scroll only when their own content exceeds that viewport. “Calm” and “restrained” constrain distraction and avoidable operations rather than imposing a low favorite count.

Exact Home insets, spacing, typography, item geometry, edit controls, and normal/edit-mode boundary preservation are defined only in the [Home presentation specification](../presentation/home.md).

## Time, date, and weekday

- Time is presented above the date and uses tabular numerals where the selected system font supports them so minute changes do not shift the clock width.
- Time follows the system 12-hour or 24-hour preference and does not show seconds.
- Date and weekday are displayed below time and follow the active locale, for example `Sat, Aug 15` in English. Use logical `start` alignment rather than a fixed physical-left offset.
- Selecting the visible time text opens the main surface of the system Clock application when it is exposed. Avenor does not hard-code a vendor Clock package. If the resolved Clock application does not expose a main launchable surface, Avenor falls back to its system alarm destination; if neither destination is available, it shows localized failure feedback without crashing.
- Selecting the visible date-and-weekday text invokes an implicit system Calendar destination without targeting a package.
- The complete time and date-and-weekday row targets follow the Home presentation specification.

### Blank-space double tap

- The basic-information module remains full width within Home's content bounds and preserves eligible blank space outside its current text and other interactive targets. That blank space supports the optional double-tap lock capability defined in [double-tap-lock.md](../features/double-tap-lock.md). Future information such as weather must preserve a practical blank portion of the module for this gesture.
- The time and date-and-weekday targets are excluded. Their existing single-tap actions remain immediate and do not wait for double-tap recognition.
- The gesture is inactive unless the user has explicitly enabled the narrowly scoped Avenor accessibility service in Android system settings.

## Favorite region

- A new installation starts with no favorites and shows a concise empty-state invitation to add applications from Drawer.
- Every favorite belongs to exactly one direct-access destination: one vertical favorite list or one favorite bar. The favorite-list area is a horizontal sequence of vertical lists ordered physically from left to right, independent of locale direction. A favorite added through the existing Drawer action is appended to the current leftmost list; if none exists, that action creates one list at the default medium size. Reordering the lists changes which list is leftmost and therefore changes the default destination for later Drawer additions. Adding a favorite does not navigate to Home or enter edit mode.
- The current product supports at most two persisted vertical favorite lists. A persisted list must contain at least one favorite. Losing its final entry through removal, uninstall reconciliation, or a completed move deletes that list immediately; the remaining list, if any, expands to the complete padded favorite-list width.
- The product does not reject an addition because a list viewport is full. Overflow extends that list's scrollable content while preserving existing order.
- The same stable launchable-entry identity cannot be stored or displayed more than once within a list, across the vertical lists, or across favorite bars.
- Primary, cloned, and work-profile launchable entries remain distinct identities. They may be assigned independently, including to different favorite destinations, and must not be deduplicated solely by package name.
- Favorite display size is a list-level property. Every entry in one list uses that list's stored size, while different lists may retain different size values. Every newly created or migrated list starts at medium; edit mode lets the user change each persisted list independently to large, medium, or small through its list-level size control.
- Exact large, medium, and small item values and name treatment are defined in the [Home presentation specification](../presentation/home.md#vertical-favorite-lists).
- Neither list has a product-defined slot limit derived from the current viewport. Visible lists share one composition viewport height and maintain independent content extents and scroll positions. A list stays stationary when its content fits and scrolls vertically when it overflows without changing another list's position or scroll state.
- Icon shape and badge use the platform-provided representation consistently across Home, Drawer, and related application UI.
- Selecting an entry immediately launches it. Ordinary selection does not produce haptic feedback. Duplicate rapid activation must be suppressed.
- Long-pressing an entry produces long-press haptic feedback and opens the application action sheet defined in [app-action-sheet.md](app-action-sheet.md).
- Favorite selection and long-press participate in the Home-wide upward-drag arbitration defined in [navigation.md](../navigation.md). An upward drag that takes ownership does not launch the favorite, open its action sheet, or produce the favorite's long-press feedback.
- A favorite is removed automatically only after a successful inventory refresh confirms that its application was uninstalled, its clone was removed, or that specific launchable identity permanently disappeared.
- An inventory loading failure never deletes or hides saved favorites.
- An application that still exists but is disabled remains in its stored position as a visibly disabled favorite. Selecting it does not attempt a normal launch and provides a short localized unavailable Toast. Long-press remains available for applicable information or management actions.
- A transient launch failure retains the favorite, provides a short localized error Toast, and refreshes that entry's state.
- A later successful refresh restores an available entry in place without changing its favorite order.

### Favorite persistence loading and failure

- A favorite persistence read is successful only when Avenor can reliably interpret the complete stored favorite state. An unreadable or damaged result is not an empty favorite list and must not show the new-installation empty state.
- When a readable saved state predates list-level size, preserve every valid favorite's existing list membership and order, assign medium to each non-empty migrated list, and omit any empty list. Migration must not duplicate or silently discard a valid favorite.
- Loading, unreadable, and fully empty favorite states are shared states of the complete favorite composition and are presented once rather than duplicated per list. An empty persisted list cannot exist: losing its final entry deletes it and causes the remaining visible-list widths to be recalculated.
- While the initial read or a user-requested retry is in progress, the favorite region shows a progress indicator and the localized message `Loading favorites…`. It does not show favorite rows, the empty-state invitation, an error icon, or a Retry action concurrently.
- If the state cannot be reliably read, the favorite region hides the progress indicator, shows the shared non-interactive status icon, displays the localized message `Unable to load favorites`, and provides a separate `Retry` action.
- The failure state preserves the original unreadable data and must not initialize, migrate, repair, clear, replace, or otherwise overwrite it. Process recreation and device restart do not reinterpret the failure as an empty list.
- While the failure or retry state remains active, all add-favorite, remove-favorite, and edit-mode mutations are disabled. Home time and date, Home-to-Drawer navigation, Drawer inventory, and application launching remain available.
- Selecting Retry starts one read-only reload, changes the favorite region to the loading presentation, and disables repeated Retry activation until that attempt completes. Retry does not write, repair, migrate, clear, or replace stored favorite data.
- A successful retry restores the exact readable favorite state and its order without an additional write. A reliably read empty state shows the normal empty-state invitation and restores favorite mutations.
- A failed retry returns to the same persistent failure presentation, restores the Retry action, preserves the original unreadable data, and keeps favorite mutations disabled. It does not add a Toast because the visible favorite-region error already communicates the result. The user may retry again.
- A normal initial read occurs on a new process start. Avenor does not continuously retry, poll, or use network-state changes as a retry trigger. Any additional safe read trigger requires technical validation and must preserve the same no-write boundary.
- If a favorite mutation surface is already open when the failure is detected, close it without applying a favorite change. Repair, export, reset, backup, and restore behavior require a later product decision and technical assessment.

## Edit mode

- Edit mode is entered either from the selected favorite's Launcher actions or by long-pressing eligible blank space in the basic-information module. Eligible blank space excludes the time, date-and-weekday, and every other interactive target. Once that long-press is recognized, it produces the standard long-press haptic feedback, enters edit mode, and suppresses double-tap locking and every other action from the same pointer sequence. The action-sheet entry remains labeled as editing rather than ordering because the mode supports ordering, list assignment, and adding favorites.
- In edit mode, every vertical-list and secondary-favorite-bar entry displays a trailing drag handle and a physical-top-left remove badge. The modules and favorite bars receive the editing surfaces defined by the [Home presentation specification](../presentation/home.md#edit-presentation). These surfaces communicate boundaries and do not imply that basic information is editable.
- Every persisted vertical list receives the presentation-defined control bar at the top of its existing viewport. In physical right-to-left order its controls reorder the complete list, select that list's size, and remove the complete list. A provisional list has no control bar. With one persisted list, reorder is hidden and non-interactive while its position remains allocated; with two it is visible. These physical positions do not mirror in RTL locales.
- The remove, size, and reorder controls use the visuals and compact-target exceptions defined by the Home presentation specification. The size control contains only the current localized value. Reorder state remains visible throughout an active drag. Ordinary remove-list and size-control activation has no haptic feedback.
- The control bar does not change the favorite-list area's external height or move adjacent Home modules. It occupies the presentation-defined top portion of each persisted list's edit-mode viewport, stays fixed while applications scroll below it, and never overlays an application. Exiting edit mode restores the complete application viewport and preserves meaningful scroll position. A provisional list remains top-aligned with a neighboring persisted list but can use its complete viewport for add-only content.
- Edit mode preserves each list's selected large, medium, or small item presentation. Application drag handles remain inside items and must not increase item height or remeasure a list; exact artwork and target geometry are defined by the Home presentation specification.
- Every application-item remove control has a localized `Remove favorite` accessibility name, occupies the physical top-left without adding an action column, preserves identifying artwork and platform badges, and never overlaps the trailing handle. Its exact compact visual and target geometry are defined by the Home presentation specification.
- Edit mode disables ordinary favorite launch and long-press action-sheet behavior. Pressing an application's remove badge immediately removes and persists that favorite. Every application move, complete-list reorder, and complete-favorite-bar reorder starts only from its visible drag handle after the platform-standard long-press threshold is recognized. Recognition produces exactly one standard long-press semantic haptic feedback and gives that gesture exclusive drag ownership; only subsequent movement creates and moves the drag preview. Release before recognition performs no drag action. Movement that begins before recognition cancels the drag candidate and remains ordinary scrolling on the owning list or favorite bar when that viewport can scroll, so a horizontal favorite-bar swipe never creates a drag preview. Movement elsewhere also remains ordinary overflow scrolling. This activation rule does not apply to Home–Drawer navigation, ordinary scrolling, or other non-edit-mode gestures. Application handles share the Home presentation defined resource and target geometry.
- After a successful edit-mode removal, Home shows a Material Snackbar with `Removed from favorites` and an `UNDO` action. The Snackbar uses the platform long-duration semantic adjusted for applicable accessibility timeout settings, does not block continued Home editing, and retains only the most recent removal snapshot while visible.
- Selecting `UNDO` restores the exact removed identity, destination, position, stored list size, and affected container order. If removing the final entry deleted its vertical list or favorite bar, Undo recreates that container at its former position and restores its properties atomically. Undo itself provides ordinary press feedback without haptic feedback.
- A later removal replaces the active Snackbar and permanently discards the earlier removal's Undo snapshot. Snackbar dismissal, leaving edit mode, the Android system Home action, process recreation, or leaving Avenor discards the snapshot; no undo history remains. If Undo persistence fails, Avenor resolves to the last successfully persisted favorite state and shows the existing favorite-update failure feedback rather than claiming restoration.
- If any identity contained by the active Undo snapshot is successfully added to another favorite destination before Undo is selected, that successful addition remains and immediately invalidates the complete Undo snapshot. Avenor dismisses the stale Snackbar without another Toast, Snackbar, Dialog, or movement back to the former destination. A cancelled or failed addition does not invalidate Undo.
- When the active Undo snapshot would recreate a vertical favorite list or favorite bar that disappeared through the removal, that removed container continues to occupy one place within its applicable two-list or five-favorite-bar limit until Undo is restored, replaced, dismissed, or otherwise invalidated. Home does not offer a provisional container that would consume the reserved place, while additions to already persisted destinations remain available. Undo therefore restores the exact removed container without exceeding either limit; releasing the snapshot releases that place immediately.
- An application-handle drag uses an independent follow-pointer preview. The preview preserves the grab offset and moves continuously across the complete Home safe content area. Persisted and provisional vertical lists and favorite bars are application destinations. The preview retains the source presentation throughout the gesture; after a successful cross-container drop, the application adopts the target list's stored size or the favorite bar presentation.
- The source application's original layout slot remains stable for the entire drag and preserves its source geometry. The slot may retain an inert representation and never contains two interactive favorites. The dragged favorite does not participate in position animation, and target changes or other-item animations must not move, jump, resize, or rebound the preview.
- Within one container, entering another application's effective body immediately exchanges the two visible positions and persists that exchange. A container is one vertical favorite list or one favorite bar. Further eligible-body crossings may perform and persist further exchanges during the same drag. Same-container gaps never accept insertion or show an insertion line. An invalid release retains the last successfully persisted same-container order rather than reverting earlier exchanges.
- When the touch point enters a different application container, two mutually exclusive target types are available. An existing application body shows an exchange highlight or border without an insertion line. A valid first, between-item, last, or provisional-empty boundary shows an insertion line without an exchange highlight. Neither cross-container operation changes visible membership before release. Release over the current valid target applies and persists exactly one exchange or insertion; insertion removes the application from its source and preserves the target's previous relative order.
- For example, with the left list `[A, B, C]` and right list `[D, E, F]`, releasing `A` over `E` exchanges the entries and produces left `[E, B, C]` and right `[D, A, F]`. Releasing `A` over the gap between `D` and `E` instead inserts it and produces left `[B, C]` and right `[D, A, E, F]`.
- Target classification uses the finger touch point, not the preview center or edge. Moving between an exchange body and an insertion boundary switches feedback without saving cross-container data. During horizontal or vertical edge auto-scroll, feedback is recalculated from the current target-container geometry and remains tied to the touch point.
- Application-level dragging uses a `48dp` auto-scroll trigger zone inside each scrollable application viewport: top and bottom for a vertical list, and left and right for a favorite bar. Remaining within a valid zone for approximately `120ms` starts scrolling in that direction. Speed increases continuously as the touch point approaches the edge; exact minimum speed, maximum speed, and interpolation are implementation-calibrated values. Leaving the zone stops scrolling immediately without requiring release. Reaching the content boundary stops scrolling without looping, overscroll, haptic feedback, or explanatory messaging.
- Only the valid container currently under the touch point may auto-scroll. The source container may scroll while it remains the current container; after entering another vertical list or favorite bar, only that target scrolls on its own axis. List gaps, module gaps, invalid space, the vertical-list control bar, and fixed favorite bar rails stop all auto-scroll. Auto-scroll applies only to application dragging, not complete-list or complete-favorite-bar reordering.
- Entering an eligible edge zone begins a short fade-in of the [Home presentation](../presentation/home.md#drag-and-overflow-feedback) edge cue during the stabilization interval; active auto-scroll holds it at its maximum presentation. Leaving the zone, changing direction, cancellation, or loss of scrollability fades the old cue out promptly. A newly eligible opposite edge begins its own stabilization and fade-in. The cue has no arrow, text, ripple, accessibility node, or haptic response. Insertion and exchange feedback remain above it, and target feedback is recalculated while content moves.
- Invalid release after entering another container restores the source application's last successfully persisted container and position. If any immediate same-container exchanges occurred earlier in the same gesture, their last successfully persisted order remains. If persistence fails, restore the last successfully saved assignments and order for identities that remain valid, retain any newer confirmed inventory reconciliation, do not claim completion, and show the existing favorite-update failure feedback.
- System Back and the Android system Home action both exit edit mode. If either occurs during an active drag, first cancel the unsaved target, remove its preview and feedback, restore the last successfully persisted visible state, and invalidate any later release from that gesture. Already persisted same-container exchanges remain. Exiting then removes editing surfaces and controls without undoing completed changes. Back exits to normal Home; the Android system Home action also resolves to normal Home under the existing Launcher navigation contract.
- The editing surfaces, visible drag handles, and visible add controls indicate that edit mode is active. No additional Home title, banner, completion button, or in-surface exit action is defined.

### Selecting a vertical-list size

- Selecting the center size control opens a dark-theme Material anchored single-selection menu. Its position derives from the tapped control's current on-screen coordinates rather than a fixed screen location. It prefers below the control, flips above when needed, and otherwise clamps inside safe content without scrolling, cropping, or moving underlying Home content. Exact placement offsets belong to the Home presentation specification.
- The menu contains large, medium, and small rows in that order. Every row uses one fixed physical left-to-right sequence that does not mirror for RTL locales: selection indicator, bundled Android-default generic application icon, then localized size text. The non-failing packaged icon is decorative, carries no badge or identity, and is never read from a favorite or Android at runtime. Exact row and preview sizes belong to the Home presentation specification.
- The indicator, icon, text, and remaining row space form one selection target and one accessibility node. Only the complete row receives a bounded menu-item ripple; neither the indicator nor preview icon is independently interactive or focusable. The current row is selected without relying on color alone. The menu uses the platform Material dark surface, light corner treatment, and default elevation without a custom border; its width accommodates the longest localized row and is equal for all three items.
- The size control's accessibility name includes the current complete value, such as `Favorite list size, Medium` or `收藏列表尺寸，中`. Selecting its current row closes the menu without writing, feedback, or layout work. Selecting another row saves the list-level size immediately, closes the menu, and performs one ordinary relayout without scale animation, haptic feedback, Toast, or Snackbar. Tapping outside, pressing system Back, or selecting the size control again closes the menu without changing the value; Back closes only the menu and leaves edit mode active. While open, the menu blocks underlying list scrolling, application dragging, complete-list dragging, and other Home edit actions.
- Relayout keeps the favorite-list area's external height and the list's width unchanged and does not affect another list's size, order, or scroll position. It preserves the identity of the previously topmost visible application as the new topmost visible application and retains its offset from the application viewport top when valid, otherwise clamping to the nearest valid offset. A larger layout becomes scrollable when it overflows; a smaller layout that now fits stops scrolling and clamps to its valid range. A persistence failure restores the last successfully saved size and corresponding layout and shows the existing favorite-update failure feedback.

### Removing and reordering vertical lists

- Selecting `Remove favorite list` opens a platform-default Android Dialog rather than a BottomSheet. Its title is `Remove favorite list?`, body is `All applications in this list will be removed from favorites.`, left action is `Cancel`, and destructive right action is `Remove`. The Dialog does not enumerate application names or counts. While open, it blocks Home scrolling, dragging, and other edit actions. Cancel, system Back, or outside-tap dismissal closes it without mutation.
- Confirming `Remove` closes the Dialog, removes and persists the complete list, and shows `List removed` with `UNDO`. The latest-only removal lifecycle applies: Undo atomically restores the former physical position, stored list size, complete membership, and application order; a later removal or any existing dismissal condition invalidates that snapshot. Removing the left list makes the remaining list the leftmost default Drawer destination, and Undo restores the prior order. Removing the final list does not create an empty replacement. Any remaining list immediately expands to the complete favorite composition width.
- A complete-list drag follows the shared handle long-press activation rule and starts only from the visible list-level reorder target. Before recognition it shows only the opacity-based pressed state; release performs no action, while movement cancels the candidate without creating a preview. Recognition produces the single standard long-press semantic haptic feedback and arms exclusive drag ownership; the first subsequent movement creates the preview and begins moving it instead of scrolling the list or starting application-level dragging.
- The complete list uses a frozen, non-interactive static-layout preview of the source list's currently visible viewport. It preserves the original width, visible height, content, scroll position, and grab offset; it does not include content outside that viewport and exposes no independent accessibility node. The source retains an equal-width, equal-height placeholder. This contract requires the observable static preview and does not require a screenshot or a specific Compose implementation.
- Target classification follows the finger touch point. Entering the other persisted list's body shows complete-list target highlighting, immediately exchanges the two physical left-to-right positions, persists the new order, and produces a short movement haptic step. List gaps never insert or show an insertion line. Further body crossings may exchange again. Invalid release, system Back, or the Android system Home action removes the preview and feedback while retaining the last successfully persisted exchange; the latter actions then follow their existing edit-mode exit behavior.

### Adding favorites in edit mode

- Edit mode places one horizontally centered add control at the real end of every persisted vertical favorite list. Each existing favorite bar also ends with one add control. The control scrolls with its owning list or favorite bar, is never a favorite or drag target, and is not persisted.
- Add controls are localized plain-text actions with a leading text `＋`, not a separate icon asset or decorative control. Persisted destinations use `＋ Add apps`; provisional destinations name the container they create. The complete localized label is the visible action and one semantic target; assistive technology need not announce the decorative plus. Target geometry belongs to the Home presentation specification and does not stretch or remeasure favorite entries.
- If no vertical favorite list exists, edit mode presents one provisional list spanning the complete padded favorite-list width and containing only its add control. If exactly one persisted list exists, edit mode presents that list and one provisional list at equal widths; the persisted list therefore previews the two-list layout. Confirming at least one application creates the provisional list at the default medium size. Cancelling or leaving edit mode removes it without persistence. Two persisted lists suppress the provisional-list entry.
- If fewer than five favorite bars exist, edit mode also presents one provisional empty favorite bar after the existing favorite bars. It contains only an add control. Normal Home never displays this provisional favorite bar, and reaching five persisted favorite bars removes the provisional-favorite-bar entry.
- A provisional vertical-list or favorite-bar entry is also omitted when an active Undo snapshot reserves the only otherwise available place of that container type.
- Selecting an add control records its exact target list or favorite bar and opens Drawer in the favorite multi-selection mode defined by [drawer.md](drawer.md). The Drawer transition completes upward programmatically; Home-to-Drawer dragging remains disabled in edit mode.
- Confirmed applications are appended to the captured target in displayed selection order. Confirming at least one application from a provisional list or favorite bar creates and persists that destination atomically with its initial entries. Cancelling, confirming no entries, or leaving through the Android system Home action does not create an empty destination.
- Opening Drawer multi-selection captures the scroll position of every Home vertical list and favorite bar in the current edit context. After ordinary cancellation through Cancel, system Back, or valid downward dismissal, Drawer completes the reverse transition and restores every captured Home container to its exact prior position without automatic scrolling; Drawer scrolling never changes those Home positions. If intervening reliable inventory reconciliation makes an exact offset invalid, restoration preserves the former topmost visible identity and offset when that identity remains, otherwise clamps to the nearest valid position. After successful confirmation, every non-target container follows the same restoration rule. If the first newly appended identity that remains valid is not fully visible, only the target container performs the minimum vertical or horizontal scroll needed to reveal that complete entry; it does not force start alignment. If that entry is already fully visible, no target scroll occurs. A newly persisted provisional container begins at its content start, naturally showing its first entry. Multiple additions remain in selection order, but only the first still-valid new entry is guaranteed complete visibility. If all newly selected identities are absent by return, no container scrolls. This reveal adds no highlight, Snackbar, or haptic feedback. The Android system Home action cancels an uncommitted selection, exits edit mode, and resolves to normal Home without retaining this temporary edit viewport; an atomic save already in progress instead follows the Drawer saving contract and is not cancelled.

- A drag beginning on a favorite's handle owns item movement. A vertical drag elsewhere inside a favorite list scrolls that list when it overflows; a horizontal drag elsewhere inside a favorite bar scrolls that favorite bar when it overflows. Edit mode never changes pages or reveals a collapsed list.
- Same-container application exchanges save when the exchange occurs. Releasing over a valid different-container target finalizes and saves one cross-container exchange or insertion according to the mutually exclusive target rules above.
- Edit mode disables Home-to-Drawer dragging. Item dragging, list scrolling, and automatic edge scrolling retain exclusive ownership until edit mode ends.

## Secondary favorites area

- The secondary favorites area is a conditional Home module below the favorite-list area with presentation-defined separation.
- It uses the complete padded Home content width rather than participating in the vertical-list width distribution.
- It has zero height when it has no content and does not reserve an empty placeholder.
- Home itself does not scroll vertically because of this area. The area contains up to five vertically stacked horizontal favorite bars. Five is the fixed current product limit; a future user-configurable favorite-bar-count setting is outside the current contract.
- Each favorite bar contains a finite, user-defined sequence of launchable application entries. It stays stationary while entries fit, scrolls horizontally when they overflow, and never scrolls cyclically. Container, entry, and spacing values belong to the Home presentation specification.
- Each favorite bar's horizontal scroll position follows the shared restoration lifecycle in [navigation.md](../navigation.md): meaningful same-process positions are retained across ordinary Avenor and external-application returns, edit-mode exit, and the Android system Home action; Drawer multi-selection uses its captured restoration and target-only minimum-reveal rules; process recreation resets every favorite bar to its content start. If intervening content changes invalidate an exact position, Avenor preserves the formerly first visible identity and its offset along the bar when possible, otherwise clamps to the nearest valid position.
- Each secondary-favorite entry uses the fixed favorite-bar presentation and content-measured width defined in the [Home presentation specification](../presentation/home.md#favorite-bars). Every locale measures rendered label width rather than literal character count. This presentation has no user size control; the large, medium, and small menu applies only to vertical favorite lists.
- Entries align from the start of each favorite bar. Shorter names produce narrower items, while unused width remains unallocated rather than redistributed. Normal and edit modes preserve calculated width; edit controls may cause earlier ellipsis but never expand an item.
- In edit mode the populated secondary-favorites module and each favorite bar use their Home presentation defined editing treatment. A scrollable favorite bar shows a short conditional fade in each direction with more content; that fade disappears at the boundary. Partial edge content may remain visible as the primary overflow cue. A favorite bar that does not overflow shows no fade and does not scroll.
- Each favorite bar becomes three fixed horizontal regions in edit mode: a logical-start remove rail, a central horizontally scrolling application viewport, and a logical-end reorder rail. Both rails are complete interaction targets, remain fixed while applications scroll, and use the geometry defined in the Home presentation specification. With one persisted favorite bar, end-reorder presentation and interaction are hidden while its rail remains allocated. A provisional favorite bar shows neither favorite-bar-level control. At least two persisted favorite bars show reorder on every persisted favorite bar; returning to one hides it without changing rail geometry.
- The start control is named `Remove favorite bar`. Selecting it opens the same platform-default Android confirmation mechanism as complete-list removal. The Dialog title is `Remove favorite bar?`, body is `All applications in this favorite bar will be removed from favorites.`, left action is `Cancel`, and destructive right action is `Remove`. It does not enumerate applications or counts and blocks underlying Home edit actions. Cancel, system Back, or outside-tap dismissal closes it without mutation. Confirming closes the Dialog, removes and persists the complete favorite bar, then shows `Favorite bar removed` with `UNDO`. Undo follows the same latest-only Snackbar lifecycle and atomically restores the favorite bar's position, complete membership, and application order; restoring the former horizontal scroll offset is not required.
- The visible end favorite-bar handle follows the shared long-press activation rule. Before recognition, horizontal movement cancels the candidate and remains ordinary favorite-bar scrolling. Once recognized, dragging reorders favorite bars vertically. The opaque, non-interactive static-layout preview freezes the complete visible favorite-bar container, including fixed rails, visible full or partial entries, boundary, and applicable overflow fades. It preserves original geometry, scroll position, content, and grab offset; off-window content is not rendered, and the preview cannot scroll, receive input, or expose accessibility nodes. The source retains an equal-size placeholder. This observable preview does not prescribe a screenshot or Compose implementation.
- Reorder target classification uses the finger touch point. Entering another persisted favorite bar body highlights the complete target, immediately exchanges the two favorite bars, persists their order, and produces a short movement haptic step; favorite bar gaps do not insert or show an insertion line. Further body crossings may exchange again. Invalid release, system Back, or the Android system Home action removes preview and feedback while retaining the last successfully persisted favorite bar order; the latter actions then follow existing edit-mode exit behavior. Application-handle targets and fixed favorite bar controls remain distinct and never overlap.
- Favorite-bar item background, boundary, corner, and press values belong to the Home presentation specification.
- The area does not classify favorite bars or entries into product-defined types. Its content is user-defined and consists of launchable application entries matching the current favorite-entry model. Current favorite bars display no title, reserve no title row or height, and provide no naming or renaming action. A future theme setting for whether titles are shown is an additive capability outside the current contract.
- Secondary favorites follow the same unique-assignment rule as vertical favorite lists. Assigning an identity to a favorite bar changes that identity's single destination rather than creating a second favorite copy.
- Secondary favorites are added through the edit-mode favorite bar add controls above. An identity already assigned to any favorite destination is unavailable for duplicate selection. Existing identities can move or exchange between favorite bars and vertical lists through the global same-container and cross-container drag contract above.
- Selecting a secondary favorite entry immediately launches its application. Long-pressing it produces long-press haptic feedback and opens the application action sheet defined in [app-action-sheet.md](app-action-sheet.md).
- Each favorite bar has an explicit first and last application entry followed by its edit-mode-only add control. Confirming applications from the provisional favorite bar creates a new favorite bar. A favorite bar is deleted immediately when it loses its final application entry through application removal, movement, or inventory reconciliation; this automatic empty-container cleanup does not open the complete-favorite-bar confirmation Dialog. Favorite bar naming remains outside the current contract.
- Unallocated transparent space below the visible modules is not the secondary favorites area.

### Inventory changes during edit mode

Any inventory event that ends edit mode also cancels an active drag, removes its preview and target feedback, and does not commit an uncompleted move or swap.

| Inventory event | Current behavior |
| --- | --- |
| A new application is installed | Keep edit mode; favorites are unchanged |
| A non-favorite application is removed | Keep edit mode; favorites are unchanged |
| A favorite's name changes | Update the row in place and keep edit mode |
| A favorite's icon or platform badge changes | Update the row in place and keep edit mode |
| A favorite application is uninstalled | End edit mode, remove the favorite, preserve already saved moves, and notify the user |
| A favorite clone is removed | End edit mode, remove that clone favorite, preserve already saved moves, and notify the user |
| A favorite application is disabled | End edit mode, retain it as a disabled favorite in its saved position, preserve already saved moves, and notify the user |
| Favorite identity can no longer be confirmed | End edit mode, retain the saved entry and order pending refresh, and notify the user |
| The full application inventory fails to load | End edit mode, retain all favorites and their saved order, and notify the user |
| The favorite count falls below two without another inventory rule requiring exit | Keep edit mode, including with zero favorites, so add controls remain available |

- Use one localized message for a confirmed favorite change: `Favorites changed. Edit mode ended.`
- Use one localized message for an inventory or identity refresh failure: `Unable to update application status. Edit mode ended.`
- Present both messages as short Toasts.

## Acceptance intent

- One vertical favorite list uses the complete Home favorite-list width; two divide it equally after the presentation-defined gap. Each list scrolls only when its own content exceeds the shared viewport, and losing its final entry deletes it and recalculates remaining widths. A favorite bar likewise deletes automatically when it loses its final application entry.
- The secondary favorites area appears only when populated, uses the complete Home content width, and contains at most five horizontal favorite bars under the Home presentation specification. Each contains user-defined launchable entries, scrolls only on overflow, and never wraps from last entry to first. Selecting an entry launches it; long-pressing opens the application action sheet.
- Every saved launchable-entry identity appears at most once across all vertical favorite lists and favorite bars. Primary, cloned, and work-profile entries remain independently assignable identities even when they share a package family.
- Loading, unreadable, and fully empty favorite states are presented once across the complete favorite composition; persisted empty lists do not exist.
- Outside edit mode, Home-to-Drawer dragging remains available from the favorite region according to its scroll-boundary handoff and from every other Avenor-managed Home element without producing the element's click or long-press action.
- Primary and cloned entries remain visibly distinguishable when the platform supplies a badge; Avenor-specific fallback distinction is outside the current scope.
- Returning to Home during the same process preserves meaningful scroll state for every vertical favorite list and favorite bar; process recreation follows [navigation.md](../navigation.md).
- An unreadable favorite state remains distinguishable from a valid empty list, cannot be overwritten through favorite actions, and can recover through a successful read-only Retry without blocking independent application discovery and launch paths.
