# Home Interaction Specification

> Public semantic source: English. Chinese counterpart: [home.zh-CN.md](home.zh-CN.md). Shared navigation is defined in [navigation.md](../navigation.md); double-tap locking is defined in [double-tap-lock.md](../features/double-tap-lock.md); shared visual rules are defined in [design-foundations.md](../design-foundations.md); the complete spatial sketches are the shared [normal Home](../wireframes/home.txt) and [Home edit mode](../wireframes/home-edit-mode.txt) wireframes, with reading rules in the [wireframe index](../low-fidelity-wireframes.md).

## Purpose and structure

Home is the Launcher primary surface. It is one fixed, non-pageable, non-collapsible surface without folders. The page itself does not scroll; only populated vertical favorite lists can scroll independently under the overflow rules below. Its content is arranged from top to bottom:

1. Basic information region: time, then date and weekday.
2. Favorite-list area: up to two equal-status vertical favorite lists containing accepted saved launchable entries.
3. Secondary favorites area: a conditional module below the favorite-list area.

Home uses content-driven vertical sizing rather than fixed proportional regions and never scrolls vertically as a whole. The basic-information region wraps its required content and interaction targets. When the natural heights of the favorite-list area and a populated secondary favorites area, including their `16dp` separation, fit within the remaining safe height, both use their natural heights and no favorite list scrolls vertically. When their combined natural height exceeds that boundary, the secondary favorites area retains its natural ribbon height and the favorite-list area uses the remaining viewport above it; only an overflowing favorite list scrolls vertically inside that viewport. The secondary favorites area uses the full available Home content width and has zero height when it has no content. Each ribbon handles its own horizontal overflow without moving other Home regions. Any remaining screen space is unallocated transparent Home space, not a persistent product region.

The favorite-list area currently supports at most two persisted vertical lists. One list uses the complete available composition width. Two lists divide the width equally after the composition's `8dp` internal padding on each side and `16dp` inter-list gap. Width is derived from the number of visible lists rather than a fixed percentage, so the current rule does not define or authorize a third list. Both lists have equal product status, share one viewport height, preserve independent content and scroll state, and scroll only when their own content exceeds that viewport. “Calm” and “restrained” constrain distraction and avoidable operations rather than imposing a low favorite count.

Home content uses `16dp` horizontal and vertical container padding. Adjacent visible Home regions use `16dp` spacing. These are internal layout distances rather than external margins because they define spacing within Home's available bounds.

## Time, date, and weekday

- Time is left-aligned above the date and uses a `57sp` font size, `64sp` line height, and bold weight. It uses tabular numerals where the selected system font supports them so minute changes do not shift the clock width.
- Time follows the system 12-hour or 24-hour preference and does not show seconds.
- Date and weekday are displayed below time with an `8dp` start inset relative to the time text, use a `16sp` font size with `24sp` line height and normal weight, and follow the active locale, for example `Sat, Aug 15` in English. The inset affects the visible text only; the complete date-and-weekday row remains its `48dp` focusable touch target. Use logical `start` alignment rather than a fixed physical left offset.
- Selecting the visible time text opens the main surface of the system Clock application when it is exposed. Avenor does not hard-code a vendor Clock package. If the resolved Clock application does not expose a main launchable surface, Avenor falls back to its system alarm destination; if neither destination is available, it shows localized failure feedback without crashing.
- Selecting the visible date-and-weekday text invokes an implicit system Calendar destination without targeting a package.
- The time line is at least `64dp` high and does not require a separately enlarged target beyond its rendered line region.
- The date-and-weekday row is `48dp` high and vertically centers its text. The complete row is its focusable touch target.

### Blank-space double tap

- The basic-information module remains full width within Home's content bounds and preserves eligible blank space outside its current text and other interactive targets. That blank space supports the optional double-tap lock capability defined in [double-tap-lock.md](../features/double-tap-lock.md). Future information such as weather must preserve a practical blank portion of the module for this gesture.
- The time and date-and-weekday targets are excluded. Their existing single-tap actions remain immediate and do not wait for double-tap recognition.
- The gesture is inactive unless the user has explicitly enabled the narrowly scoped Avenor accessibility service in Android system settings.

## Favorite region

- A new installation starts with no favorites and shows a concise empty-state invitation to add applications from Drawer.
- Every favorite belongs to exactly one direct-access destination: one vertical favorite list or one secondary-favorite ribbon. A favorite added through the existing Drawer action is appended to the first favorite list; if none exists, that action creates the first list at the default medium size. Adding a favorite does not navigate to Home or enter edit mode.
- The current product supports at most two persisted vertical favorite lists. A persisted list must contain at least one favorite. Losing its final entry through removal, uninstall reconciliation, or a completed move deletes that list immediately; the remaining list, if any, expands to the complete available width.
- The product does not reject an addition because a list viewport is full. Overflow extends that list's scrollable content while preserving existing order.
- The same stable launchable-entry identity cannot be stored or displayed more than once within a list, across the vertical lists, or across secondary-favorite ribbons.
- Primary, cloned, and work-profile launchable entries remain distinct identities. They may be assigned independently, including to different favorite destinations, and must not be deduplicated solely by package name.
- Favorite display size is a list-level property. Every entry in one list uses that list's stored size, while different lists may retain different size values. The current product exposes no size-selection interaction: every newly created or migrated list uses medium. Large and small remain defined presentation tokens but are not user-selectable in the current interaction contract.
- Large uses a `48dp` application icon, `64dp` item height, and a normal-weight `18sp/28sp` name. Medium uses a `40dp` icon, `56dp` item height, and a normal-weight `16sp/24sp` name. Small uses a `32dp` icon, `48dp` item height, and a normal-weight `14sp/20sp` name. Every size uses `8dp` item padding where its geometry allows, a `16dp` icon-to-name gap, system font scaling, and one static end-ellipsized name line without marquee behavior.
- Neither list has a product-defined slot limit derived from the current viewport. Visible lists share one composition viewport height and maintain independent content extents and scroll positions. A list stays stationary when its content fits and scrolls vertically when it overflows without changing another list's position or scroll state.
- Icon shape and badge use the platform-provided representation consistently across Home, Drawer, and related application UI.
- Selecting an entry immediately launches it. Ordinary selection does not produce haptic feedback. Duplicate rapid activation must be suppressed.
- Long-pressing an entry produces long-press haptic feedback and opens the application action sheet defined in [app-action-sheet.md](app-action-sheet.md).
- Favorite selection and long-press participate in the Home-wide upward-drag arbitration defined in [navigation.md](../navigation.md). An upward drag that takes ownership does not launch the favorite, open its action sheet, or produce the favorite's long-press feedback.
- A favorite is removed automatically only after a successful inventory refresh confirms that its application was uninstalled, its clone was removed, or that specific launchable identity permanently disappeared.
- An inventory loading failure never deletes or hides saved favorites.
- An application that still exists but is disabled remains in its stored position as a visibly disabled favorite. Selecting it does not attempt a normal launch and provides a short localized unavailable Toast. Long-press remains available for applicable information or management actions.
- A transient launch failure retains the favorite, provides a short localized error Toast, and refreshes that entry's state.
- A later successful refresh restores an available entry in place without changing its favorite order.

### Favorite persistence loading and failure

- A favorite persistence read is successful only when Avenor can reliably interpret the complete stored favorite state. An unreadable or damaged result is not an empty favorite list and must not show the new-installation empty state.
- When a readable saved state predates list-level size, preserve every valid favorite's existing list membership and order, assign medium to each non-empty migrated list, and omit any empty list. Migration must not duplicate or silently discard a valid favorite.
- Loading, unreadable, and fully empty favorite states are shared states of the complete favorite composition and are presented once rather than duplicated per list. An empty persisted list cannot exist: losing its final entry deletes it and causes the remaining visible-list widths to be recalculated.
- While the initial read or a user-requested retry is in progress, the favorite region shows a progress indicator and the localized message `Loading favorites…`. It does not show favorite rows, the empty-state invitation, an error icon, or a Retry action concurrently.
- If the state cannot be reliably read, the favorite region hides the progress indicator, shows a non-interactive `40dp` error icon, displays the localized message `Unable to load favorites`, and provides a separate `Retry` action.
- The failure state preserves the original unreadable data and must not initialize, migrate, repair, clear, replace, or otherwise overwrite it. Process recreation and device restart do not reinterpret the failure as an empty list.
- While the failure or retry state remains active, all add-favorite, remove-favorite, and edit-mode mutations are disabled. Home time and date, Home-to-Drawer navigation, Drawer inventory, and application launching remain available.
- Selecting Retry starts one read-only reload, changes the favorite region to the loading presentation, and disables repeated Retry activation until that attempt completes. Retry does not write, repair, migrate, clear, or replace stored favorite data.
- A successful retry restores the exact readable favorite state and its order without an additional write. A reliably read empty state shows the normal empty-state invitation and restores favorite mutations.
- A failed retry returns to the same persistent failure presentation, restores the Retry action, preserves the original unreadable data, and keeps favorite mutations disabled. It does not add a Toast because the visible favorite-region error already communicates the result. The user may retry again.
- A normal initial read occurs on a new process start. Avenor does not continuously retry, poll, or use network-state changes as a retry trigger. Any additional safe read trigger requires technical validation and must preserve the same no-write boundary.
- If a favorite mutation surface is already open when the failure is detected, close it without applying a favorite change. Repair, export, reset, backup, and restore behavior require a later product decision and technical assessment.

## Edit mode

- Edit mode is entered either from the selected favorite's Launcher actions or by long-pressing eligible blank space in the basic-information module. Eligible blank space excludes the time, date-and-weekday, and every other interactive target. Once that long-press is recognized, it produces the standard long-press haptic feedback, enters edit mode, and suppresses double-tap locking and every other action from the same pointer sequence. The action-sheet entry remains labeled as editing rather than ordering because the mode supports ordering, list assignment, and adding favorites.
- In edit mode, every favorite displays a drag handle. Each currently defined editable Home module receives the shared translucent light-gray, small-rounded-corner editing surface defined in [design-foundations.md](../design-foundations.md). In the current product this means the basic-information and favorite-list-area modules; the secondary favorites area's editing surface is not defined by this contract. These surfaces communicate module boundaries and do not imply that the basic-information module is editable.
- Edit mode preserves each list's selected large, medium, or small item height. Every list uses the same `24dp` drag-handle graphic. Each handle's hit region is `24dp` wide plus `12dp` horizontal padding on each side (`48dp` total) and follows the current item's full height. The graphic has no additional right margin; visible spacing comes from the item's existing padding. Handles remain inside rows and must not increase row height or remeasure a list.
- A handle drag uses an independent follow-pointer preview. The preview preserves the grab offset and moves continuously across the complete Home safe content area, including both lists and the space between them. Only persisted or provisional vertical favorite lists accept these currently defined drops; other Home areas allow the preview to pass through but do not accept it. The dragged favorite does not participate in list-position animation, and target changes or other-item animations must not move, jump, or rebound the preview.
- The source favorite's original layout slot remains stable for the entire drag and preserves the source row height, so the list does not shrink, the shared viewport does not change, and other favorites do not shift because the source was temporarily removed. Before an exchange target is selected, the slot may retain an inert representation of the source; when an in-list exchange target moves there, that target replaces or covers the inert source representation. The slot never contains two interactive favorites. In-list exchange may update visible positions during the drag; cross-list exchange and insertion show target feedback without changing either list until release. Saved state is finalized only when the current valid operation is released.
- While the touch point remains in the source list, the only operation is exchange. Entering another favorite's effective body makes that favorite the exchange target; the target favorite immediately exchanges positions with the source favorite during the drag, while the independent preview remains controlled by the pointer and does not join the target animation. If the touch point changes to another eligible body, the visible exchange updates to that target. Gaps in the same list never show an insertion line and never accept insertion. Touching the dragged favorite, the source position, or an invalid area produces no new exchange.
- When the touch point enters the other list, two mutually exclusive target types are available. An existing favorite body means cross-list exchange: the target receives an exchange highlight or border without an insertion line, but neither list changes its visible position or assignment during the drag. A valid gap means cross-list insertion: an insertion line marks the target boundary, no exchange border appears, and neither list changes until release. On release, the current cross-list exchange or insertion is applied once; insertion removes the dragged favorite from the source list and places it at the boundary while preserving the target list's relative order. The first boundary, between-item boundaries, last boundary, and an empty provisional target list are all valid insertion targets.
- For example, with list 1 `[A, B, C]` and list 2 `[D, E, F]`, releasing `A` over `E` exchanges the entries and produces list 1 `[E, B, C]` and list 2 `[D, A, F]`. Releasing `A` over the gap between `D` and `E` instead inserts it and produces list 1 `[B, C]` and list 2 `[D, A, E, F]`.
- Target classification uses the finger touch point, not the preview center or edge. The preview may cover a target visually without changing its type. Moving from an exchange body to an insertion gap switches the border to an insertion line; moving back switches to exchange feedback. No target switch changes saved data. During edge auto-scroll, feedback is recalculated from the current target-list geometry and remains tied to the touch point; scrolling or list switching must not cause an unexplained target jump.
- In-list exchange updates the visible positions as the touch point enters eligible favorite bodies. Cross-list exchange and insertion remain feedback-only until release and do not change either list during the drag. On release over the current valid target, apply the active operation once and save it. An invalid release restores the original source position and list without changing saved state. The dragged preview remains independent; in-list exchange transitions may animate the target favorite, but cross-list feedback must not move the preview or either list. If persistence fails, restore the last successfully saved list assignments and order for favorites whose identities remain valid, then retain any newer confirmed inventory reconciliation result. Failure recovery must not restore an application, clone, or launchable identity that a later inventory event confirmed as removed or invalid. Do not present the operation as completed, and show the existing favorite-update failure feedback.
- System Back and the Android system Home action both exit edit mode. If either occurs during an active drag, first cancel that drag, remove its preview and target feedback, restore the last saved visible list and order, and invalidate any later release from that gesture so it cannot save. Exiting then removes the editing surfaces, drag handles, and add controls without undoing moves or additions completed before exit. Back exits to normal Home; the Android system Home action also resolves to normal Home under the existing Launcher navigation contract.
- The editing surfaces, visible drag handles, and visible add controls indicate that edit mode is active. No additional Home title, banner, completion button, or in-surface exit action is defined.

### Adding favorites in edit mode

- Edit mode places one horizontally centered add control at the real end of every persisted vertical favorite list. Each existing secondary-favorite ribbon also ends with one add control. The control scrolls with its owning list or ribbon, is never a favorite or drag target, and is not persisted.
- Each add control uses the standard `24dp` plus graphic in an interaction target at least `48dp` wide and high. Its row or ribbon retains the owning list's or ribbon's normal height; the control does not stretch or remeasure favorite entries.
- If no vertical favorite list exists, edit mode presents one full-width provisional list containing only its add control. If exactly one persisted list exists, edit mode presents that list and one provisional list at equal widths; the persisted list therefore previews the two-list layout. Confirming at least one application creates the provisional list at the default medium size. Cancelling or leaving edit mode removes it without persistence. Two persisted lists suppress the provisional-list entry.
- If fewer than five secondary-favorite ribbons exist, edit mode also presents one provisional empty ribbon after the existing ribbons. It contains only an add control. Normal Home never displays this provisional ribbon, and reaching five persisted ribbons removes the provisional-ribbon entry.
- Selecting an add control records its exact target list or ribbon and opens Drawer in the favorite multi-selection mode defined by [drawer.md](drawer.md). The Drawer transition completes upward programmatically; Home-to-Drawer dragging remains disabled in edit mode.
- Confirmed applications are appended to the captured target in displayed selection order. Confirming at least one application from a provisional list or ribbon creates and persists that destination atomically with its initial entries. Cancelling, confirming no entries, or leaving through the Android system Home action does not create an empty destination.
- After confirmation or ordinary cancellation, Drawer completes the reverse transition downward. Confirmation returns to the same Home edit mode and preserves its meaningful scroll context. Ordinary cancellation also returns to that edit mode without changing favorites. The Android system Home action cancels the selection and exits edit mode to normal Home.

- A drag beginning on a favorite's handle owns item movement. A vertical drag elsewhere inside a favorite list scrolls that list when it overflows. Edit mode never changes pages or reveals a collapsed list.
- Releasing a dragged favorite finalizes the current in-list exchange, cross-list exchange, or cross-list insertion when the current target is valid, then immediately saves it. Cross-list release follows the mutually exclusive target, preview, invalid-drop, and save rules above.
- Edit mode disables Home-to-Drawer dragging. Item dragging, list scrolling, and automatic edge scrolling retain exclusive ownership until edit mode ends.

## Secondary favorites area

- The secondary favorites area is a conditional Home module below the favorite-list area, separated from it by `16dp` vertical spacing.
- It uses the full available Home content width rather than participating in the vertical-list width distribution.
- It has zero height when it has no content and does not reserve an empty placeholder.
- Home itself does not scroll vertically because of this area. The area contains up to five vertically stacked horizontal ribbons. Five is the fixed current product limit; a future user-configurable ribbon-count setting is outside the current contract.
- Each ribbon is `56dp` high, adjacent ribbons use `8dp` vertical spacing, and each ribbon contains a finite, user-defined sequence of launchable application entries. A ribbon stays stationary while its entries fit and scrolls horizontally when they exceed its visible width; ribbons do not scroll cyclically.
- Each secondary-favorite entry displays a `40dp` platform application icon and one static application-name line within a `128dp`-wide item. Its height, icon size, `16dp` icon-to-name gap, and `16sp/24sp` normal-weight name match the medium favorite-list size. The icon is the primary recognition cue and the name is a secondary visual label. A name that does not fit uses end ellipsis.
- Entries are left-aligned within each ribbon with `8dp` between adjacent fixed-width items. Remaining horizontal space stays unused rather than being redistributed among the items.
- Each item uses the light foreground semantic color at `6%` opacity as a subtle translucent background, plus a `1dp` boundary using that semantic color at `12%` opacity and a `12dp` corner radius. This treatment keeps the transparent Home background perceptible while making the item's bounds understandable; ordinary press feedback continues to use the current light ripple contract.
- The area does not classify ribbons or entries into product-defined types. Its content is user-defined and consists of launchable application entries matching the current favorite-entry model. Whether a ribbon displays a title and how that title behaves are `To be decided`; the current contract does not require or prohibit one.
- Secondary favorites follow the same unique-assignment rule as vertical favorite lists. Assigning an identity to a secondary ribbon changes that identity's single destination rather than creating a second favorite copy.
- Secondary favorites are added through the edit-mode ribbon add controls above. An identity already assigned to any favorite destination is unavailable for duplicate selection. The interaction for moving an existing identity between a secondary ribbon and a vertical favorite list remains outside the currently defined boundary.
- Selecting a secondary favorite entry immediately launches its application. Long-pressing it produces long-press haptic feedback and opens the application action sheet defined in [app-action-sheet.md](app-action-sheet.md).
- Each ribbon has an explicit first and last application entry followed by its edit-mode-only add control. Confirming applications from the provisional ribbon creates a new ribbon. A ribbon is deleted immediately when it loses its final application entry through removal or inventory reconciliation. Ribbon naming, movement of existing members, and ribbon ordering remain outside the current contract.
- Unallocated transparent space below the visible modules is not the secondary favorites area.

### Inventory changes during edit mode

Any inventory event that ends edit mode also cancels an active drag, removes its preview and target feedback, and does not commit an uncompleted move or swap.

| Inventory event | Current behavior |
| --- | --- |
| A new application is installed | Keep edit mode; favorites are unchanged |
| A non-favorite application is removed | Keep edit mode; favorites are unchanged |
| A favorite's name changes | Update the row in place and keep edit mode |
| A favorite's icon or platform badge changes | Update the row in place and keep edit mode |
| A favorite application is uninstalled | End edit mode, remove the favorite, preserve already saved moves, and notify the user |
| A favorite clone is removed | End edit mode, remove that clone favorite, preserve already saved moves, and notify the user |
| A favorite application is disabled | End edit mode, retain it as a disabled favorite in its saved position, preserve already saved moves, and notify the user |
| Favorite identity can no longer be confirmed | End edit mode, retain the saved entry and order pending refresh, and notify the user |
| The full application inventory fails to load | End edit mode, retain all favorites and their saved order, and notify the user |
| The favorite count falls below two without another inventory rule requiring exit | Keep edit mode, including with zero favorites, so add controls remain available |

- Use one localized message for a confirmed favorite change: `Favorites changed. Edit mode ended.`
- Use one localized message for an inventory or identity refresh failure: `Unable to update application status. Edit mode ended.`
- Present both messages as short Toasts.

## Acceptance intent

- One vertical favorite list uses the full composition width; two lists divide available width equally after fixed padding and inter-list spacing. Each list scrolls only when its own content exceeds the shared viewport, and losing its final entry deletes it and immediately recalculates remaining widths. A secondary ribbon likewise deletes automatically when it loses its final application entry.
- The secondary favorites area appears below the favorite-list area only when it has content, uses the full available Home content width, and contains at most five `56dp`-high horizontal ribbons of user-defined launchable application entries. Each `128dp`-wide entry shows a `40dp` icon and one static application-name line, ribbons scroll horizontally only when their content overflows, and reaching a ribbon's last entry does not wrap to its first. Selecting an entry launches its application, and long-pressing it opens the existing application action sheet.
- Every saved launchable-entry identity appears at most once across all vertical favorite lists and secondary ribbons. Primary, cloned, and work-profile entries remain independently assignable identities even when they share a package family.
- Loading, unreadable, and fully empty favorite states are presented once across the complete favorite composition; persisted empty lists do not exist.
- Outside edit mode, Home-to-Drawer dragging remains available from the favorite region according to its scroll-boundary handoff and from every other Avenor-managed Home element without producing the element's click or long-press action.
- Primary and cloned entries remain visibly distinguishable when the platform supplies a badge; Avenor-specific fallback distinction is outside the current scope.
- Returning to Home during the same process preserves its meaningful list state; process recreation follows [navigation.md](../navigation.md).
- An unreadable favorite state remains distinguishable from a valid empty list, cannot be overwritten through favorite actions, and can recover through a successful read-only Retry without blocking independent application discovery and launch paths.
